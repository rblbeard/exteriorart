Free teaching videos by Randy Beard on swimming pool construction defects, code compliance (Title 24 and NEC Article 680) and design. Each page has the video, a short summary and the full transcript. Attorneys, plan checkers and builders are welcome to cite or share them.

Construction Defects & Forensic Analysis

The failure patterns behind a large share of pool and aquatic construction disputes — what went wrong, and the written standard it violated.

  • Super El Niño 2026 Update: 60% Odds It’s the Strongest Since 1950. Don’t Drain Your Pool.
    NOAA’s November 12, 2026 update, and what a strong El Niño winter does to six kinds of pool owner: flat-ground pools, hillside pools, pools under construction, contracts about to be signed, winter replasters, and disputes where the evidence changes after the first storm. Odds, not a rain forecast.
  • The Wheelbarrow Problem: Why Concrete Pool Repairs Fail
    Jackhammer out the shotcrete, epoxy in new steel with no deputy inspector, then mix concrete in a wheelbarrow and hand-pack the wall. It happens constantly — and it violates ACI 562, ACI 506, and CBC Chapter 17 special inspection at every step.
  • The Anatomy of a Hidden Defect
    When a pool is re-plastered, the demolition method quietly decides how long the new finish lives. Impact tools drive interconnected micro-cracks — “bruising” — into the shell. You can’t see it, tapping can’t find it, and the new finish bonds to it anyway.
  • The $20,000 Pool Waterproofing Mistake
    A $4 million home, a custom perimeter-overflow pool, and within one year: glass tile delaminating and efflorescence bleeding through. The root cause is almost always waterproofing treated as a product instead of a system.
  • The Nano-Silica Deception
    Liquid densifiers and colloidal silicate waterproofers are everywhere in pool work, and the safety data tells a different story than the marketing — respirable mist, nano-silica dust, and particles small enough to pass a standard dust mask.
  • The House Burned. Is the Pool Still Good?
    A pool full of water acts as a heat sink, so after a wildfire the shell below the waterline often survives while the bond beam, coping, tile and deck take the heat. What to look at, and what testing tells you whether the pool can be saved.
