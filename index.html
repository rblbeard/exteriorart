About Randy Beard

Randy Beard has worked in the swimming pool trade since 1979 — 47 years. He started out servicing and repairing pools, then spent decades building and running a full pool construction company of more than 60 employees, with its own masonry, tile, plumbing, and steel crews. That hands-on building background is the basis of his forensic work today.

Randy served for several years as an investigator for the California Contractors State License Board (CSLB) and conducted plaster inspections for the National Plasterers Council for several years. He was chapter president of the Independent Pool and Spa Service Association (IPSA) for multiple terms, and has taught hydraulics to city building inspectors and pool equipment and water chemistry to Orange County Health Department inspectors.

FAQs

Common Attorney Inquiries

Answering key structural and procedural questions for counsel assessing Randy's qualifications.

What are your primary areas of expertise as a pool expert witness?

Randy Beard's core areas of litigation support include swimming pool structural defects, hydraulic design failures, soils/geotechnical hillside pool interfaces, pool plaster inspections and failures, safety/compliance reviews, and National Electrical Code (NEC Article 680) requirements for pool grounding and bonding.

Do you handle both plaintiff and defense assignments?

Yes. Randy provides unbiased forensic evaluations for defense counsel, insurers, HOAs, property owners, and plaintiff attorneys. His findings are based on industry standards, building codes, and physical evidence.

What geographical areas do you serve?

Based in Costa Mesa (Orange County, Southern California), Randy serves the Los Angeles, San Diego, and Riverside metropolitan areas. He is also available for site inspections, depositions, and trial testimony nationwide.

Why is NEC Article 680 compliance critical in swimming pool lawsuits?

NEC Article 680 governs electrical installations in and around swimming pools, hot tubs, and spas. Improper bonding (equipotential bonding grids) or incorrect grounding of pumps and lights creates severe shock and electrocution hazards. Randy evaluates bonding and grounding compliance in aquatic personal injury and construction defect cases.
